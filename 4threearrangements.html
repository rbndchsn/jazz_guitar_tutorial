Three Arrangements; Examples

One progression, worked three ways. The chords were already fine — what was missing was a melody, a place for the first cadence to land, and a reason for the second half to feel different from the first. Each version below keeps the original material and changes one thing at a time.

The vamp underneath all three: Cmaj7 and Fmaj7 trading bars six times over, then Dm7–G7, Em7–A7, Dm7–G7, and home. Bars printed in red are where each version does its work.

How to work through these

Learn version one first and stay there until the melody is automatic. It uses chords you already know and changes nothing but the top string — and it is the version that teaches the actual skill. Versions two and three are colour on top of that skill, not replacements for it.

Then the rhythmic part, which no diagram can show: play the chord change an eighth note early, tied over the barline, on maybe two changes out of four. Bass note on beat one, upper strings on the and of two. That single habit will do more for these twelve bars than any substitution on any of these four pages.
